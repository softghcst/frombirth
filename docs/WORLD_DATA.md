# World data: what is real, what is game, and what still needs checking

Pathways has 103 places to live: the 102 on the reference list, plus Ghana, which was already in the game.
This page says where each kind of information about them comes from and how far it can be trusted.

## Three kinds of statement

| Kind | Examples | Where it lives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reference** | name, ISO codes, kind of place, parent, capital, languages, time zone, currency code, form of government, cities | `js/data/world.js`, `js/data/countries.js`, `js/data/worldNew.js` | Compiled by hand; **not yet verified** line by line |
| **Gameplay estimates** | pay and price levels, tax rates, the 0–100 living scores, hazards, the currency `scale` | `js/data/living.js`, `js/data/countries.js`, `js/data/worldNew.js` | Deliberately simplified. Not statistics. |
| **Your world** | who governs, election results, laws passed, royal houses | the save (`state.polities`, `state.dynasties`) | Fiction, generated by play |

The World Explorer shows them in separate sections and says which is which.

## Sources and verification

Nothing here was machine-imported. The reference rows were written from general knowledge of these standards:

- **Codes and regions**: ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 and alpha-3; UN M49 regions (<https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/overview/>).
- **Currency codes**: ISO 4217 (<https://www.iso.org/iso-4217-currency-codes.html>).
- **Time zones**: IANA tz database names (<https://www.iana.org/time-zones>). One principal zone per place.

Every row in `world.js` therefore carries `verification: 'compiled'`. To verify a place: check its row against the sources
above, then set `verification: 'verified'` for it. `node tests/world.mjs --report` lists what is unverified or thin, place by place.

No city coordinates, populations or statistics are stored: none could be sourced with a clear licence in this pass, and inventing
them was not an option.

## Things that are deliberately not what they look like

- **`currency.scale` is a fictional simulation rate**, roughly the order of magnitude of each currency against a base unit so that
  prices read naturally. It is not a market quote and does not move with real exchange rates.
- **Law flags** (same-sex marriage, LGBT protection, gambling, the death penalty, conscription) are the author's best understanding of
  the mid-2020s for the 48 newest places and have not been checked against legislation. They configure the game; they are not legal
  information. Laws change, and in a long game the simulation changes them too.
- **"Free health care" and "free university"** are yes-or-no simplifications of systems that are neither.
- **Browsing groups follow a game convention**, not strict geography: Georgia and Turkey are listed under "Middle East", and Central
  America and the Caribbean under "North America". The UN region is stored separately as `unRegion`.

## Places that are not ordinary sovereign states

| Place | Modelled as | Tied to | Nationality at birth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puerto Rico | territory | United States | United States |
| Aruba | territory (constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands) | Netherlands | Dutch |
| Hong Kong | special administrative region | China | Chinese |
| Taiwan | self-governing jurisdiction, no parent; own currency and passports | — | its own |

Taiwan's status is disputed; the game lists it as a distinct place to live and takes no position. The Democratic Republic of the
Congo (COD, Kinshasa) is not the Republic of the Congo, which is not in the game. Each of these has a one-sentence neutral `status`
shown on its profile.

## Known gaps (as of this pass)

- The 48 newest places use the default school model, short name lists (8–10 names each) and first-estimate gameplay numbers.
- No subdivisions (states, provinces) or city-level differences yet: a city is a name within its country.
- Citizenship rules (`js/data/citizenship.js`) are three yes-or-no lists and a table of years: which places grant citizenship by
  birth, which allow only one nationality, and how long a resident waits. They are unverified simplifications of laws that have
  conditions and exceptions. Grandchildren and people the game invents have a nationality inferred from where they are from.
- There is one kind of visa. Work, study and family routes, and temporary statuses, are not built yet.
- Everyday money is one balance in the currency of the country of residence; other currencies are separate accounts. Property and
  loans are still valued in the everyday currency and converted when you move. Exchange rates are fictional and only drift within
  bounds: they do not follow each country's inflation.

## Adding a place

Add one row to `NEW_PLACES` in `js/data/worldNew.js` and one row to `ROWS` in `js/data/world.js`, then run `node tests/world.mjs`.
Nothing else needs to change: ids are stable two-letter strings, never positions in a list.
