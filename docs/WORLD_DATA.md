# World data: what is real, what is game, and what still needs checking

Pathways has 103 places to live: the 102 on the reference list, plus Ghana, which was already in the game.
This page says where each kind of information about them comes from and how far it can be trusted.

## Three kinds of statement

| Kind | Examples | Where it lives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reference** | name, ISO codes, kind of place, parent, capital, languages, time zone, currency code, form of government, cities | `js/data/world.js`, `js/data/countries.js`, `js/data/worldNew.js` | Compiled by hand; **not yet verified** line by line |
| **Gameplay estimates** | pay and price levels, tax rates, the 0–100 living scores, hazards, the currency `scale` | `js/data/living.js`, `js/data/countries.js`, `js/data/worldNew.js` | Deliberately simplified. Not statistics. |
| **Your world** | who governs, election results, laws passed, royal houses | the save (`state.polities`, `state.dynasties`) | Fiction, generated by play |

The World Explorer shows them in separate sections and says which is which.

## Sources and verification

The reference rows were written by hand, then checked field by field by `scripts/verify-world.py` against:

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| ISO codes, standard name | ISO 3166-1, via [pycountry](https://pypi.org/project/pycountry/) |
| Currency in use | ISO 4217 as used by the [Unicode CLDR](https://cldr.unicode.org/), via [babel](https://pypi.org/project/babel/) |
| Official languages | Unicode CLDR territory data, via babel |
| Time zone | the [IANA time-zone database](https://www.iana.org/time-zones), via [tzdata](https://pypi.org/project/tzdata/) |
| Capital, continent (as a check on the UN M49 region), cities, the states of American cities | [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) (CC BY 4.0), via [geonamescache](https://pypi.org/project/geonamescache/) |

The script reads the game's data through Node, so the JavaScript stays the only copy. It writes the results to
`js/data/worldVerification.js` (generated: do not edit it), and each place carries them as `checked`. The World Explorer shows
them under "How this was checked". Each field is one of:

- **confirmed**: the source agrees;
- **differs on purpose**: the source disagrees and a person decided the game's value stands. The reason is in `REVIEWED` in the
  script. Examples: Taiwan is named "Taiwan" rather than ISO's "Taiwan, Province of China"; Astana is the capital of Kazakhstan
  although the GeoNames extract still says Nur-Sultan; Ukrainian is Ukraine's only official language although CLDR adds Russian;
- **not confirmed**: the source has no answer. Ten towns in nine places are absent from the GeoNames extract, which is
  incomplete (it lacks El Alto, a city of nearly a million), so this is not evidence that they do not exist.

As of this pass, 94 places are fully confirmed and 9 partly, and nothing disagrees with a source unexplained. `node
tests/world.mjs` fails if anything does. To re-check after changing the data:

```
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install pycountry babel tzdata geonamescache
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify-world.py --write
```

The packages are for checking only; the game itself still has no dependencies.

### Laws, ages, citizenship and government

`scripts/verify-laws.py` checks these against English Wikipedia's comparison articles, each fetched at a pinned revision
(listed in `SOURCES` in the script), so a check can be repeated against exactly the same text. Wikipedia is a secondary
source; it is used because it gathers one table per question for every country, and its rows cite the primary sources
(ILGA World, Amnesty International, national statutes). Every disagreement was decided by a person and the reason written in
`DECIDED`; nothing was copied across automatically. Results are in `js/data/lawVerification.js` (generated), shown in the World
Explorer under "How the law was checked", and `node tests/rules.mjs` fails if anything disagrees unexplained.

| Question | Source article | Test used |
|---|---|---|
| Death penalty | Capital punishment by country | in use = an execution in the last ten years |
| Same-sex marriage | Same-sex marriage (timeline of legalizations) | legal nationwide |
| LGBT people | LGBTQ rights in Africa / Asia / Europe / the Americas / Oceania | hostile = activity criminalized; protected = civil unions or marriage, and anti-discrimination law |
| Military service | Conscription (by-country table) | compulsory in law, even where selective |
| Voting, drinking, pension ages; alcohol bans | Voting age; Legal drinking age; Retirement age | pension age = the men's age, or the standard age where a range is given |
| Divorce | Divorce law by country | |
| Citizenship by birth | Jus soli | unconditional, allowing for a diplomats' exception or a registration step |
| One nationality only | Multiple citizenship, and 13 national nationality-law articles | |
| Form of government | List of countries by system of government | |
| Marriage age | Marriageable age | without parental or judicial consent; a separate age for women where the law sets one. The game never lets anyone marry before 18 |
| Driving age | List of minimum driving ages | the youngest age to hold a car license, conditions allowed; not mopeds, motorcycles or supervised learners |
| Gambling age, and whether gambling is legal | Gambling age | the casino age where several are given; legal where lotteries or betting are open to citizens |
| School-leaving age | School-leaving age | the end of compulsory schooling |
| Residence before naturalization; dual nationality | Naturalization | |
| Free movement | Member state of the European Union; Trans-Tasman Travel Arrangement | the European area is the EU members in the game, plus Norway (EEA) and Switzerland |

What changed as a result: four countries no longer execute (the Bahamas, DR Congo, Ethiopia, Uganda); six gained "protected"
status (Bolivia, Czechia, Croatia, Italy, Monaco, Nepal); conscription was added in twelve countries and removed in Syria;
voting ages for Greece, Taiwan, Samoa and Nepal; drinking ages for six countries and an alcohol ban for Pakistan; pension ages
for 64 countries, with the default lowered from 68 to 65; the United Arab Emirates, Afghanistan and Ukraine now allow dual
nationality; Malaysia and Singapore require ten years' residence; five forms of government were relabeled.

Also checked now: the top rate of income tax ("List of countries by tax rates"; 92 agree, and four differ for reasons recorded in
the script: the United States uses the federal 37%, and Bolivia, Estonia and Romania use their flat income tax where the table adds
other charges), health-care models ("Universal health care": no country the source lists as universal is out of pocket or
private in the game), and the spouse's shorter wait for citizenship, now set per country where the "Naturalization" notes give it
(`SPOUSE_YEARS_IN`; elsewhere the old rule: half the usual wait, at least three years).

Estate and inheritance tax (`js/data/inheritance.js`) is now by heir: a spouse, a child, or anyone else, each with their own
tax-free amount and scale, from PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (pages reviewed in 2026) and Wikipedia's "Inheritance tax". The
script checks every country against PwC's chart of headline rates: 73 agree, 6 differ for recorded reasons (Brazil uses São
Paulo's 4%; Chile, Spain and Japan include the surcharge on heirs outside the family; Tunisia and Ukraine), and 24 are not
confirmed: 15 are not in the chart, and 9 have no figure there (Belgium, Switzerland, Finland, Poland and Venezuela give only
words; Guatemala, Kenya, Morocco and Peru are "not provided"). Each entry's `src` says whether its rates and allowances are
stated by a source, partly sourced (the range or top rate is, the bands between are the author's), or the author's alone.

Regional law is now checked by hand against Wikipedia ("Capital punishment in the United States", "Gambling age", "Legal
drinking age", "Alcohol laws of India", "Gambling in India", "Sharia in Nigeria" and the driving-license articles for the United
States, Canada and Australia). Each claim in `REGIONAL_POWERS` is marked in `js/data/rules.js` as confirmed or as the author's
understanding. This added the casino age by state and province (19 in Ontario, British Columbia and Nova Scotia; 18 in Georgia,
New York and Washington; 21 in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg), a drinking age of 25 in Delhi and 18 in Karnataka, 21 in Dubai,
Abu Dhabi and Ajman, and the ban on alcohol around Kano. It let Australian and Mexican states set their own drinking age, and
corrected Malaysia's drinking age to 21 and Paraguay's to 20. The script's drinking-age check had let these pass, because it
accepted an age mentioned anywhere in a row, including a former age in its notes. It now reads only the age columns and skips
ages that were changed. A second round confirmed Canada's and Spain's regional gambling and the US drinking age, and
found the United Arab Emirates' gambling is now federal (a national regulator since 2023), so it is no longer the emirates'
to decide. Left as the author's judgment, with no source found: regional gambling in Argentina, Malaysia, Nigeria,
Australia and Germany, and Austria's drinking age. National governments cannot change what is left to the regions.

Income tax: which countries tax at one flat rate is checked against the lowest and highest rates in "List of countries by
tax rates" (92 agree; Bolivia, Estonia, Romania and Ukraine are flat taxes the table mixes with allowances or contributions;
Belize was made flat, at its 25%). Whether a banded country's bands rise steeply or moderately is the author's judgment.
The six status sentences (Aruba, Puerto Rico, Andorra, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Hong Kong, Taiwan) were read
against the Wikipedia article on each place (revs 1384847800, 1386424195, 1384796794, 1385995928, 1386426160, 1386443146)
and each claim in them is supported. Where a source does not cover a place (often a small country or a territory), the
field is "not confirmed", not guessed.

Re-run with `python3 scripts/verify-laws.py --write` (needs access to en.wikipedia.org; pages are cached in `.cache/`).


No city coordinates, populations or statistics are stored.

## Things that are deliberately not what they look like

- **`currency.scale` is a fictional simulation rate**, roughly the order of magnitude of each currency against a base unit so that
  prices read naturally. It is not a market quote and does not move with real exchange rates.
- **Law flags and legal ages** are checked against published summaries (above) but are one value per country: real law has
  exceptions, regional variation and dates of effect that are not modeled. They configure the game; they are not legal
  information. Laws change, and in a long game the simulation changes them too.
- **"Free health care" and "free university"** are yes-or-no simplifications of systems that are neither.
- **Browsing groups follow a game convention**, not strict geography: Georgia and Turkey are listed under "Middle East", and Central
  America and the Caribbean under "North America". The UN region is stored separately as `unRegion`.

## Places that are not ordinary sovereign states

| Place | Modeled as | Tied to | Nationality at birth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puerto Rico | territory | United States | United States |
| Aruba | territory (constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands) | Netherlands | Dutch |
| Hong Kong | special administrative region | China | Chinese |
| Taiwan | self-governing jurisdiction, no parent; own currency and passports | — | its own |

Taiwan's status is disputed; the game lists it as a distinct place to live and takes no position. The Democratic Republic of the
Congo (COD, Kinshasa) is not the Republic of the Congo, which is not in the game. Each of these has a one-sentence neutral `status`
shown on its profile.

## Known gaps (as of this pass)

- The 48 newest places use the default school model, short name lists (8–10 names each) and first-estimate gameplay numbers.
- City profiles (`js/data/cities.js`) are two multipliers, housing and pay, guessed by the author for twelve countries and
  generic elsewhere (the capital, and the first city listed, assumed to be dearer and better paid). They come from no dataset.
  Region names are real and unverified. Regions exist only where a city in the game sits in one; they have no government,
  taxes or budget of their own. Safety, schools and health care vary only by kind of city (capital, largest, other), by a rule in `SERVICES`, not by data about any place. People the player knows are not paid by city.
- Citizenship rules (`js/data/citizenship.js`) are three yes-or-no lists and a table of years: which places grant citizenship by
  birth, which allow only one nationality, and how long a resident waits. They are unverified simplifications of laws that have
  conditions and exceptions. Grandchildren and people the game invents have a nationality inferred from where they are from.
- The moving world (`js/data/economy.js`) is fiction with invented odds. An economy has three states and no numbers behind
  them (no growth, unemployment or interest rates). Wars can end, and can begin (rarely, and only within a country whose stability is very low; no war is ever between two named countries). Stability, safety and the other living
  scores do not change. Only the player's country of residence feels its economy; other countries' phases are news, and
  nudge where people move and what currencies do. Elections abroad are not simulated. People who move are named in the
  journal but have no city, visa or job search of their own.
- Legal rules (`js/data/rules.js`) hold one value per place for fourteen questions. Ages are the common adult case and ignore
  exceptions (marriage with consent, beer before spirits, learner permits). Where a real country differs by state or province
  the value is the commonest one. Nine regional overrides exist (four American states, three Canadian provinces, one Indian state, one emirate). A head of government can change them through the legislature; public
  opinion on each is a fixed number shaded by the law the country already has, not a model of its people. Regional law can be changed by the head of a region,
  for the matters `REGIONAL_POWERS` gives each country: compiled by hand from each country's division of powers and not yet
  checked against sources. Mayors make no law. School-leaving age is the same everywhere.
- Immigration routes (`js/data/immigration.js`) are the same nine everywhere (the eight above and a retirement route), with the
  same fees, terms and five-year path to permanence. Real systems differ by country and are not modeled. The free-movement areas
  (a European area, and Australia with New Zealand) are simplified lists and unverified. Family members hold dependant status on the
  player's visa, simplified to one rule for every country; there is no deportation for crime and no overstaying. Moving abroad is a case decided
  when the year turns (`js/engine/relocation.js`); the odds of a decision are invented gameplay parameters.
- Visits (`js/data/travel.js`, `ENTRY`, `VISIT_AREAS`, `LIBERAL`, `ON_ARRIVAL`) are a hand-compiled, **unverified** picture of the
  mid-2020s: which nationalities visit which places without a visa, need an electronic authorization (ESTA, the UK's ETA, eTA,
  K-ETA, NZeTA, Australia's ETA) or need a visitor visa, the longest stay, and the Schengen area's 90 days in any 180. The visa
  names are the common ones. Real rules depend on far more than nationality (purpose, previous travel, the document held,
  exemptions and reciprocity), change often, and are not modeled. Fees, processing times and the odds of a decision are invented
  (`DECISION`), and a decision never depends on nationality. This is not travel or immigration advice.
- Professional recognition (`REGULATED`, `RECOGNITION` in `js/engine/credentials.js`) is one simplified process for every country:
  which careers are regulated, the language level each asks, the fee, and the odds of a license, an adaptation year or a refusal are
  game numbers. Real recognition differs by profession and country, and is often much slower.
- Passports are valid ten years (five for children under sixteen) everywhere; real terms vary. A passport is issued only to a
  national of the issuing place, and expiring, being lost or being replaced never changes citizenship.
- Transport (`MODES`) uses broad global dates (the first railways around 1830, liners from 1840, scheduled airlines from 1919,
  jets from 1958); real availability varied by country and route. Overland travel follows landmasses (`LANDMASS`, `ISLANDS`,
  the Channel Tunnel from 1994); every coastal place is assumed to have a port and every place an airport. `LANDLOCKED` is
  factual. Speeds and fares are game numbers. Before 1914 no passports or visas are asked for (simplified: some states did).
- Earlier jurisdictions (`JURISDICTIONS`) go back to 1800: well-known dates of independence, unification, partition and
  renaming, hand-compiled for the places in the game and **unverified**; wartime occupations are not shown. Only the name shown
  changes: nationality is not re-derived through successor-state rules. A life can begin in any year from 1800 (Historical Eras,
  `docs/HISTORICAL_ERAS.md`); travel follows it: what transport existed, the three document eras, settling abroad without papers
  before 1914, and countries at war (from the eras' events in `data/history.js`) closed to visitors.
- Coordinates (`js/data/coordinates.js`) come from GeoNames through `scripts/build-coordinates.py`: 570 of the game's cities.
  Seventeen are not in that extract and are left out (the game uses the capital's position for them, marked approximate). The
  family map draws real positions only, on Natural Earth's 1:110m land outline (public domain, generated by
  `scripts/build-land.py`), and draws lines only along moves that were recorded.
- Everyday money is one balance in the currency of the country of residence; other currencies are separate accounts. Property abroad
  is valued in its own country's money and shown at today's rate, with its mortgage and rent kept in the currency they were
  agreed in. Each country has its own housing index, moved by its economy; the numbers are invented, not house-price data. No
  country restricts foreign buyers, and there are no tax treaties: nothing is taxed twice, and nothing is credited. Income tax is
  each country's headline rate through one of four shapes (`js/data/systems.js`) with thresholds in multiples of a typical wage:
  not real brackets, and with no social contributions, credits or regional taxes. The top band is the country's real top rate (`TAX_TOP`), checked. Health care is one of six models; that no
  universal-care country is modeled as out of pocket or private is checked, but the finer choice among models is not, and quality of care and waiting are not affected by the model. Loans are in the everyday currency and converted when you move. Exchange rates are fictional: they drift within
  bounds and are pulled weaker by a recession or a crisis, without following measured inflation.

## Adding a place

Add one row to `NEW_PLACES` in `js/data/worldNew.js` and one row to `ROWS` in `js/data/world.js`, then run `node tests/world.mjs`.
Nothing else needs to change: ids are stable two-letter strings, never positions in a list.
